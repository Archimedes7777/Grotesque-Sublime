## 2. The brand name: ACTIVELYPSYCHOTIC

- It is a compound coined from two ordinary words, so it is distinctive for art and prints, but the word "psychotic" is crowded in apparel (see findings). Expect the clothing class to be the contested one.
- The domain `activelypsychotic.com` has been registered since 2025-02-06 and is parked, which suggests it is yours **[Unverified]**.
- Platform note: mental-health terms in a brand name can draw extra moderation attention (ads, shop listings). This is a platform-policy risk, not a trademark one **[Moderate]**. Keep imagery and captions clear of self-harm glorification (`.agents/product-marketing.md`).
- Strategy: file the brand word mark, and rely on the logo and the Bob Frapples names for distinctiveness in clothing.

## 3. The universe name: BOB FRAPPLES

- It is a coined word, which is the strongest kind of mark. Nothing found conflicts with it in art, clothing, or print goods.
- Watch the "Frap-" sound. Starbucks enforces "Frappuccino" hard (the search results show it winning a case against a "Dabuccino" product line). For art and apparel, confusion is unlikely, but **do not use the name on food, beverages, or drinkware imagery that looks like coffee cups** **[Moderate, my inference]**.
- Short form "Frapples" on its own is a different, thinner mark. File the full name first.

## 4. The logo: skull in a bitten apple

- Apple Inc. filed 215 trademark oppositions at the USPTO between 2019 and 2021, some against marks with only passing similarity, according to a trade-press summary. One report names an apple-with-a-bite-on-crossed-bones logo for a music festival that Apple challenged **[Unverified outcome]**.
- Your emblem has the two features Apple's mark is built on: a single bite and a leaf. The risk is highest on apparel and on anything electronic or accessory-like (phone cases, cables, stickers for laptops).
- Parody or satire is not a safe harbor when you use the design as your own brand on goods **[Moderate]**.
- Ways to lower the risk without changing the idea:
  - Keep the ornate frame and banner (they already separate it from Apple's flat silhouette).
  - Prefer skull-shaped or worm-eaten openings over a clean single bite.
  - Avoid mono-color glossy gradient apples that echo Apple's look.
  - Keep the Apple-like leaf small or change it.
  - Never use the actual Apple logo (several camera-roll images show it).

## 5. Plan

### Step 1: free knockout search (about 1 to 2 hours, you or me guiding)
1. USPTO Trademark Search (`trademarks.uspto.gov`): search `ACTIVELYPSYCHOTIC`, `ACTIVELY PSYCHOTIC`, `PSYCHO ACTIVE`, `PSYCHOTIC` (live marks in classes 16, 25, 41), then `FRAPPLES`, `FRAPPLE`, `FRAPPLS`, `BOB FRAPPLES`, plus the skull-apple design (use the design-code search for apples and skulls). Open serial 97555544 and read its status and goods.
2. Filter to the classes in the table below plus the "live" status.
3. Search the places where unregistered ("common law") users show up: Etsy, Redbubble, Amazon, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X, Facebook, Google.
4. Confirm your handles are secured on all four platforms, and check `bobfrapples` too.
5. Save screenshots with dates. This is your clearance record.

### Step 2: protect what you already own (this week)
- [ ] Confirm you own `bobfrapples.com` and `activelypsychotic.com`. Turn on **auto-renew** and **registrar lock**, and put the registrar account behind a hardware key. The 2026-12-20 expiry is the urgent item.
- [ ] Consider registering `.net`, `.shop`, and the shortened names you want, so nobody else does.
- [ ] Claim every platform handle that is free.
- [ ] Start a dated record: first public use of the name on each platform and any first sale. The first-use date matters for priority.

### Step 3: attorney review (before filing)
- Bring the knockout results. Ask for a clearance opinion on the name and a risk opinion on the logo.
- Ask about the filing basis: **use in commerce** (you already sell) or **intent to use** (you plan to).
- Cost varies by attorney and scope **[Unverified]**; ask for a fixed quote.

### Step 4: file the word marks first
- File **standard-character `ACTIVELYPSYCHOTIC`** first (it is the brand), once the attorney has cleared it against PSYCHO ACTIVE. Then **standard-character `BOB FRAPPLES`**. Standard-character marks cover any styling, so they protect you even if the logo changes.
- Do not file for classes you do not plan to use soon. The shop is not live; start with the art and entertainment classes (16 and 41) and add clothing (25) when products are real and the attorney has assessed the apparel conflicts.
- Base application fee: **$350 per class** according to several 2026 fee guides, with surcharges for incomplete information or free-text descriptions. Confirm at `uspto.gov` before filing **[Moderate]**.
- Start with the classes you actually sell. Class numbers per the USPTO's Nice classification **[Moderate]**; confirm each in the USPTO ID Manual:

| 16 | Prints, posters, stickers | "Death's Core" and "Neon Swamp" prints, "Skeletal Bloom" stickers |

- Budget example **[Estimate from the $350 base]**: one mark in classes 16 and 41 = about $700; two marks (ACTIVELYPSYCHOTIC and BOB FRAPPLES) in those two classes = about $1,400; adding clothing for both = about $2,100. Each mark and each class is a separate fee.
- File the logo later, once the design is final and the Apple-distance review is done.

### Step 5: after filing
- Use the **™** symbol now. Use **®** only after the USPTO registers the mark.
- Set a calendar reminder for the deadlines the USPTO sends; missing a response deadline can kill an application.
- Consider a watch service that alerts you to confusingly similar new filings **[Unverified cost]**.
- Keep specimens: photos of the mark on real products and live shop pages.

## 6. Related risks (not trademark, but connected)

- **Copyright in AI images:** images produced purely by AI prompts may not be copyrightable in the US **[Moderate]**. Keep your prompts, selections, edits, and paintovers as evidence of human authorship.
- **Scarcity claims:** "limited edition" must be literally true. Record edition sizes.
- **Currency design:** keep the banknote parody digital, away from real banknote size and color.
- **Franchise references:** avoid naming other brands and franchises in captions or on goods.
