# YouTube niche research: where the earning potential is (2026)

**Purpose:** rank candidate niches for a new, separate channel project (not ActivelyPsychotic) by earning potential, then pick the ones that get a filled prompt library in this folder.

**Date of research:** 2026-11-06. Figures tagged **[Measured]** come from studies that read real YouTube Studio data. Figures tagged **[Estimate]** come from vendor blogs and calculators that publish planning ranges without methodology; they disagree with each other by up to 7x for the same niche. Treat every number as [Unverified] until a channel's own YouTube Studio shows it.

**Recommendation:** start with niche 1 and keep niche 3 as the moat. Niche 1 has the best combination of measured ad RPM (it sits in the Education & Science category, the highest measured median), the highest sponsor and affiliate rates of any non-finance category, fresh topic supply, and a direct fit with the owner's prompt-engineering experience. Niche 3 has almost no competition and a credential nobody else in the niche can show, but a smaller audience ceiling. A channel that covers AI tooling for technical and clinical professionals can carry both without splitting the audience.

**What the data does not support:** the common claim that personal finance is the automatic top earner. The only measured study found a Business & Finance median RPM of $2.01, from a sample of three channels, against Education & Science at $10.22. Finance's high advertiser CPM is real, but a large share of finance views do not serve an ad, and the niche is saturated with templated faceless channels that the inauthentic-content policy now targets.

## 2. Scoring model

Seven factors, weighted, each scored 1 (worst) to 5 (best). Weights reflect that revenue per view and non-ad revenue together decide earnings, and that competition and policy risk decide whether a new channel ever reaches the views.

| Factor | Weight | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| Ad RPM evidence | 20 | Measured median RPM for the category, discounted when only estimates exist |
| Sponsor and affiliate rates | 20 | What brands pay per 1,000 views for integrations, plus affiliate payouts |
| Demand and growth | 15 | Search volume, subscriber growth velocity, topic supply |
| Competition | 15 | How crowded the niche is for a new entrant (5 = open) |
| Faceless feasibility and production cost | 10 | Can one person produce it without appearing on camera, and how many hours per video |
| Policy and risk | 10 | Inauthentic-content exposure, YMYL (your money or your life) credibility demands, AI-label prominence, licensing |
| Owner's edge | 10 | A credential, skill, or body of experience that competitors cannot copy |

## 3. Evidence by factor

### 3.1 Ad RPM (revenue per 1,000 views, after YouTube's share)

The best dataset found: AIR Media-Tech and MilX, 300 channels, 3,595 monetized channel-months, May 2025 to May 2026, medians **[Measured]**.

| Category | Median RPM | P25 to P75 | Share of views that serve an ad | Views to earn $1,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Education & Science | $10.22 | $2.31 to $19.50 | 77 percent | about 98,000 |
| Transport (7 channels) | $5.69 | $3.17 to $9.81 | | about 176,000 |
| Lifestyle | $2.98 | $1.77 to $5.24 | | about 336,000 |
| News & Politics (3 channels) | $2.60 | $1.79 to $3.62 | | about 385,000 |
| Entertainment (40 channels) | $2.43 | $0.88 to $5.02 | | about 412,000 |
| Gadgets & Tech | $2.33 | $0.70 to $3.71 | about one third | about 430,000 |
| Gaming | $2.05 | $0.70 to $3.62 | | about 487,000 |
| Business & Finance (3 channels) | $2.01 | $0.73 to $5.17 | | about 498,000 |
| Health & Sport | $1.23 | $0.72 to $1.53 | | about 810,000 |
| Kids & Teens | $0.33 | $0.16 to $0.88 | | about 3,014,000 |

Three findings matter for the ranking:

- **Education & Science is the outlier,** and its spread is the widest (8.4x between P25 and P75). The category wins because 77 percent of its views serve an ad; the channel's execution decides where in the spread it lands.
- **Tech has high advertiser CPM but low RPM** because only about a third of views serve an ad. Framing a tech channel as education (how to do a job with a tool) rather than gadget review moves it into the better category.
- **Finance and news samples are tiny** (3 channels each). Vendor estimates for finance run $8 to $25 RPM **[Estimate]**. The truth is probably between the two: finance pays well per ad but monetizes fewer views.

Shorts: a second AIR study (274 channels, updated September 2026) found **Shorts RPM is 3 to 14 percent of long-form RPM** in almost every niche, typically $0.07 to $0.20 per 1,000 views **[Measured]**. Shorts are a discovery tool, not a revenue line. Long-form over 8 minutes with mid-roll ads is where ad revenue lives.

Seasonality: Q4 CPMs run 30 to 50 percent above Q1 **[Estimate, consistent across sources]**.

Audience geography matters more than niche at the margin: a US, UK, Canada, Australia audience commands 2 to 3x the rate of the same niche with a developing-market audience. Content in English, about problems those viewers have, is a revenue decision.

TubeAI, 144,370 channels, snapshots September 7 to 27, 2026, median 30-day subscriber growth for channels with 10,000 to 100,000 subscribers **[Measured]**. Baseline across all niches: 0.97 percent.

| Niche | Median growth per 30 days |
|---|---|
| Kids content | 1.59 percent |
| ASMR | 1.56 percent |
| Creator economy and YouTube strategy | 1.50 percent |
| Challenges and stunts | 1.47 percent |
| AI and machine learning | 1.44 percent |
| True crime | 1.34 percent |
| Education and learning | 1.30 percent |
| Entertainment | 1.27 percent |
| Science | 1.20 percent |
| Gaming | 1.20 percent |

AI, education, and science all grow above baseline and sit in the best RPM category. Kids and ASMR grow fastest but monetize worst (Kids & Teens RPM $0.33, COPPA limits on personalized ads). Vendor claims of "+340 percent" or "18x" growth for AI tutorials could not be tied to any dataset and are ignored here.

Underserved topics named by more than one source **[Estimate]**: legal and tax education, senior health, menopause, men's mental health, diaspora and remote-worker tax, cybersecurity for non-experts, career development.

### 3.4 Competition

No source publishes a defensible competition metric. Ratings used here come from the consensus of vendor guides plus a judgment about how a new channel differentiates:

- **Saturated:** personal finance, true crime, motivation, gaming, beauty, general entertainment.
- **Medium and rising:** AI tools (new channels weekly; most are tool-of-the-week reviews, few are workflow depth for a profession).
- **Open:** cybersecurity for non-experts, legal and tax, career development, healthcare technology management (a search on 2026-11-06 for biomedical equipment technician content finds training snippets and vendor demos, no consistent educational channel of size).

### 3.5 Policy and risk

**Inauthentic content policy.** YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" monetization policy to "inauthentic content" on July 15, 2025. The published rule (checked on Google's own page on 2026-11-06) bars from monetization: "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production," image slideshows and scrolling text with minimal narrative, and similar videos with minimal variation. It explicitly allows AI used to express "your unique creative voice." Secondary sources report a January 2026 enforcement wave that terminated batches of mass-produced AI channels **[Unverified]**. Implication: a faceless channel is fine; a templated one is not. Original analysis, original scripts, and visible expertise are the defense.

**YMYL categories.** Finance, legal, and health content is judged by advertisers and by YouTube on credibility. A channel without a visible credential faces lower trust and higher demonetization-by-advertiser risk. Health is also a "sensitive" category for the AI-content label, which then appears on the player rather than in the description.

**Licensing.** Disaster and engineering-failure channels depend on footage. News footage, dashcam, and bystander video have owners; stock montages trip the inauthentic rule. Budget for licensed footage, public-domain government sources (NTSB, NOAA, USGS, CSB investigation animations are US federal works and generally public domain **[Moderate]**), or original animation.

**Employer and confidentiality.** A healthcare technology channel must not show identifiable devices under a specific hospital's service contract, patient data, or a current employer's internal procedures without permission.

### 3.6 Owner's edge

Stated in the session: about 8 years in healthcare technology management as a CBET-certified biomedical equipment technician, plus AI and prompt engineering experience, plus demonstrated security-policy work in this repository. That edge is strongest in niches 1, 2, and 3, absent in finance and legal, and partial in engineering failures (technical literacy, no investigative credential).

## 4. Earnings scenarios

Monthly figures, long-form only, at two view levels, US-heavy audience, after the channel passes Partner Program thresholds. Ad revenue uses the measured category median where one exists and the midpoint of estimates otherwise. Sponsorship assumes one integration per month at the low end of the niche CPM on the month's best video. All **[Estimate]**.

| Niche | 100,000 views per month | 500,000 views per month | Non-ad upside |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI tools for professionals | $600 to $1,200 ads; $1,500 to $3,000 one sponsor | $3,000 to $6,000 ads; $5,000 to $12,000 sponsor | Affiliates at $50 to $500 per referral; paid templates and prompt packs; consulting |
| Cybersecurity and privacy | $500 to $1,100 ads; $1,500 to $2,500 sponsor | $2,500 to $5,500 ads; $5,000 to $10,000 sponsor | Recurring VPN and password-manager affiliates; checklists and courses |
| Healthcare technology management | $500 to $1,000 ads; $1,000 to $3,000 flat-fee B2B sponsor | View ceiling is lower; 500,000 per month is unlikely inside 24 months | Training products, CBET exam prep, consulting to hospitals and vendors; the audience is small but has budgets |
| Personal finance | $200 to $2,000 ads (the spread is the measured range) ; $2,500 to $5,000 sponsor | $1,000 to $10,000 ads; $12,000 to $25,000 sponsor | Highest ceiling, highest competition, regulatory disclosure duties |
| Engineering failures and disasters | $300 to $800 ads; $1,000 to $2,000 sponsor | $1,500 to $4,000 ads; $5,000 to $10,000 sponsor | Low; documentary audiences buy little. Licensing costs reduce margin |

Reference point: the largest weather channel found (Ryan Hall, Y'all) shows a third-party AdSense estimate of about $39,000 per month on about 14.5 million monthly views, an implied RPM near $2.70 **[Estimate]**, consistent with the measured News & Politics median. Disaster coverage earns on volume, not rate. Framing it as engineering and science education, not news, is what raises the rate.

## 5. Platform rules the plans depend on

| Rule | Current reading | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Partner Program, full ad revenue | 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months, or 10 million valid Shorts views in 90 days; the paths are alternatives | [Unverified], third-party summaries, 2026-11-06 |
| Partner Program, early access | 500 subscribers with activity minimums; fan-funding features only | [Unverified] |
| Rumored change | Some sources mention a February 2027 revision raising the ad-revenue bar | [Unverified], future-dated |
| AI-content disclosure | Required for realistic altered or synthetic content a viewer could mistake for real; label in the description, or on the player for health, finance, news, and elections | Policy confirmed in 2024 reporting; a May 2026 update is mentioned but not found |
| Inauthentic content | Templated, mass-produced, minimal-variation content is not monetized; AI with creative vision is allowed | Confirmed on Google's policy page, dated July 15, 2025 |
