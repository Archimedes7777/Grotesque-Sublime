# Prompt library: personal finance and investing

**Niche rank:** 3 of 5, tied (score 67). See `01-niche-research.md` for the evidence.

**Thesis:** finance has the highest advertiser and sponsor rates on YouTube and the biggest audience of the five. It is included because the earning ceiling is the highest, not because it is the best fit. The owner has no stated finance credential, the niche is saturated with templated faceless channels, and it is a YMYL (your money or your life) category where credibility decides whether advertisers and viewers trust the channel.

**The only defensible angle without a finance credential:** finance for a specific professional group the owner understands (healthcare technicians and clinical staff: shift differentials, overtime, certification pay, hospital retirement plans, contract versus staff work), presented as research and personal experience, never as advice. That narrows the audience but gives the channel a reason to exist.

**Main risks:** regulatory exposure (presenting as advice, undisclosed sponsor conflicts), the inauthentic-content rule (finance is the most templated faceless category), and measured RPM data that contradicts the vendor hype (Business & Finance median $2.01 in the only measured study, from three channels).

## Context block (paste before every prompt)

```text
Context for every answer:
- Project name: [working title]. Channel handle: not created yet.
- What the channel is about: money decisions for healthcare technicians, nurses, and other hourly and salaried clinical staff: pay structures, overtime and differential math, certification return on investment, hospital retirement plans, contract versus staff work, side income, and how to evaluate financial products without being sold to. Presented as research and personal experience, explicitly not financial advice.
- Why me: about 8 years as a CBET-certified biomedical equipment technician who has lived the pay structures discussed, plus AI and prompt engineering experience for building calculators and comparison tools viewers can use. No financial credential; the channel says so.
- Target viewer: ages 22 to 50, US-based healthcare and technical workers earning $45,000 to $120,000, who search "nurse overtime tax", "hospital 403b match", "is a certification worth it", "travel contract vs staff pay". They watch personal finance channels and feel none are about their situation.
- Formats available: slides and spreadsheets on screen with voiceover (primary), screen-recorded calculators, talking head optional, Shorts cut from long-form.
- Assets on hand: first-hand knowledge of healthcare technology pay and certification economics, the ability to build and share calculators and spreadsheets, AI tooling to produce them quickly.
- Time budget: [hours] per week for research, production, and posting, by one person.
- Goal for the first 90 days: 30 long-form videos, Partner Program thresholds in progress, a free calculator as a lead magnet, no sponsor until the disclosure process is written.
- Constraints: every video carries a "not financial advice, I am not a licensed advisor" statement on screen and in the description; no specific security or product recommendations; no income claims; no affiliate for any product the owner has not used; sponsor conflicts disclosed; tax statements cited to the IRS or a primary source and dated.
- Rules: every post is a draft a person reviews and publishes by hand; no automated likes, follows, comments, DMs, or uploads; no tool receives passwords, cookies, or tokens; AI-generated or altered content gets the platform's label where its rules require it (finance is a sensitive category, so expect the prominent label if a synthetic voice is used).
- Mark [Unverified] any platform threshold, policy, tax figure, or price you cannot confirm, with the date of your knowledge.
```

## Niche-specific notes for all prompts

- **Category positioning:** education for a profession, not generic "how to get rich". The audience specificity is the inauthentic-content defense and the sponsor pitch at once.
- **Disclosure:** write the disclosure block once and reuse it in every description. Sponsorships in finance need a written policy before the first deal.
- **Calculators as products:** the spreadsheet or web calculator is the retention device, the lead magnet, and later the paid product.
- **Measured versus estimated RPM:** plan on $2 to $5 RPM, treat $10 or more as upside, and expect sponsors to be the larger line.

## Prompts in recommended order

### Step 1. Niche evaluation

```text
Act as a YouTube market researcher and channel strategist.
Using the context above, evaluate "personal finance for healthcare technicians and clinical staff" as a niche. Cover: audience size and search intent (top 20 search phrases, with which are evergreen and which are seasonal around tax and open enrollment), competition (name 5 to 8 finance channels that reach this audience and what each lacks), monetization ceiling by revenue type with the credibility limits of having no financial credential, content sustainability over 2 years, and the angle first-hand pay experience gives.
Define the ideal viewer in one paragraph, recommend 3 to 4 content categories, and give 30 video ideas ranked by expected pull, each tagged Short or long-form, with the calculation or decision it helps the viewer make.
Flag any idea that drifts into product recommendation or advice, and rewrite it as a comparison method the viewer applies themselves.
```

### Step 2. Channel plan

```text
Act as a YouTube strategist.
Using the context above, write a 12-week plan with:
- Positioning statement (one sentence) and a channel description under 1,000 characters that includes the not-advice disclosure.
- Content pillars with a target share: pay and overtime math, benefits and retirement plan mechanics, certification and career return on investment, contract versus staff decisions, and evaluating financial products without being sold to.
- Formats and lengths: which pillars are 8 to 15 minute long-form, which are Shorts.
- A publishing cadence that fits the time budget and lines up with tax season and open enrollment.
- Retention tactics for finance explainers: the number the viewer wants first, then how it was calculated, then what changes it.
- A weekly action checklist the owner follows by hand.
Leave monetization out; it is a separate prompt for a later phase.
```

### Step 3. Scripts, hooks, titles, thumbnails

```text
Act as a YouTube content strategist and editor for a finance education channel.
Using the context above, take the top 10 ideas from Step 1 and produce for each:
- 3 title options under 60 characters: specific, state the decision and the audience, no dollar-sign hype, no "secret", no all-caps.
- 1 thumbnail concept: one number or a two-column comparison with an overlay of 3 words or fewer; no cash imagery.
- The first 15 seconds as a script: the decision the viewer is facing, the number they will have by the end, and the one-line disclosure.
- A section outline with the headline number before the midpoint, the calculation and its assumptions after it, what would change the answer, and the disclosure at the end.
- One line on why a viewer stays to the end.
Plain, concrete language. No em dashes, no "it's not X, it's Y" reveals, no engagement bait, no income claims.
```

### Step 4. Analytics loop

```text
Act as a YouTube data analyst.
I will paste numbers I export by hand from YouTube Studio (impressions, click-through rate, average view duration, average percentage viewed, returning viewers, subscribers gained, traffic sources, top videos, viewer geography, RPM). You do not have account access and must not ask for it.
Build:
1. A weekly review template that takes 10 minutes: what to record, and what counts as signal versus noise at low view counts.
2. A monthly review template: which decisions, audiences, and formats worked, what to repeat, what to stop, and whether RPM is tracking the measured finance median or the vendor estimates.
3. Decision rules, for example: low click-through rate with high average view duration means change the thumbnail or title, not the video; high views from outside the target profession means the title is too generic.
4. The three metrics that matter most in the first 90 days and why the others can wait.
State the sample sizes below which a conclusion is premature.
```

### Step 5. Growth mechanics

```text
Act as a YouTube growth strategist with expertise in content discovery.
Using the context above, explain how YouTube surfaces finance explainers (search intent, suggested videos next to large finance channels, browse, the Shorts feed) in plain terms, focused on what a one-person channel can act on.
Then give a practical growth plan: upload frequency within the time budget, how to test a title or thumbnail with few views, how to capture seasonal demand (tax season, open enrollment, contract renewal cycles), how to be surfaced next to large finance channels without copying their format, which Shorts to cut from each long-form video, and how to turn a viewer into a subscriber without begging.
Rules: every engagement action is manual by the owner. No automation, follow-for-follow, engagement pods, or paid views. Mark platform mechanics you cannot verify as [Unverified] with a date.
```

### Step 6. Production workflow

```text
Act as a production manager for a one-person finance education channel.
Using the context above, design a repeatable weekly workflow from idea to published video and Shorts cut from the same material. Cover: topic selection, primary-source research (IRS publications, plan documents, Bureau of Labor Statistics pay data), building the calculator or spreadsheet, script with cited figures, screen recording, voiceover, editing, thumbnail, metadata including the disclosure block, the AI-content disclosure step (expect the prominent finance label if a synthetic voice is used), and the human review and publish step.
Constraints: the publish step is always a person in YouTube Studio; do not propose auto-upload tools. Any tool you recommend must be named with its cost [Unverified] and must not need account passwords or session cookies. Include a quality checklist (disclosure present on screen and in description, every figure cited and dated, no product recommendation, no income claim, sponsor and affiliate disclosure, AI label) and estimated minutes per step so the total fits the time budget.
```

### Step 7. Monetization sequence (run after 30 videos or 90 days of data)

```text
Act as a creator business strategist specializing in YouTube monetization for finance education, with attention to disclosure rules.
Using the context above and the analytics so far, lay out the revenue options in the order they become realistic: YouTube Partner Program (state current thresholds and mark them [Unverified] with a date), a paid calculator or spreadsheet pack, sponsorships (state a defensible CPM range, the written disclosure policy to adopt before the first deal, and the sponsor categories to decline: anything promising returns, high-fee products, anything the owner would not use), affiliates only for tools the owner uses, memberships, newsletter, and a course only if viewers ask.
For each give requirements, pros, limits, the regulatory or disclosure duty it creates [Unverified, cite the source], and the trigger that says "now is the time".
Rules: no sponsor or affiliate changes a conclusion; every paid relationship is disclosed on screen and in the description; no product the owner cannot explain the fees of.
```
