# Prompt library: AI tools and agent workflows for working professionals

**Niche rank:** 1 of 5 (score 81). See `01-niche-research.md` for the evidence.

**Thesis:** most AI channels review the tool of the week. Almost none show a working professional building a repeatable workflow with agents, prompts, and automation to do a real job faster, with the failure modes shown honestly. That is Education & Science content (the highest measured RPM category), it attracts SaaS and AI vendor sponsors at the top of the sponsor CPM table, and it is the one niche where the owner's prompt-engineering experience is the differentiator.

**Why it beats the alternatives:** fresh topic supply (new tools weekly), the strongest affiliate economics of any non-finance niche, and screen-capture production that needs no face on camera.

**Main risks:** rising competition; tool-review templates fall under the inauthentic-content rule; vendor sponsorship can bias recommendations. The defense is original workflows demonstrated end to end, with results measured on screen.

## Context block (paste before every prompt)

```text
Context for every answer:
- Project name: [working title]. Channel handle: not created yet.
- What the channel is about: a working professional shows how to build AI-assisted workflows (Claude, agents, prompt systems, automations) that remove hours from real technical and clinical jobs, with the failures shown, not just the wins.
- Why me: experienced AI and prompt engineer with about 8 years in healthcare technology management as a CBET-certified biomedical equipment technician. I have built zero-trust agent policies and skill-vetting procedures for AI tooling and can show real, non-trivial workflows rather than demos.
- Target viewer: ages 25 to 50, in the US, UK, Canada, or Australia; engineers, technicians, analysts, clinicians, and operations staff who already use one AI tool and want it to do real work. They search "how to automate X with AI", "Claude vs ChatGPT for Y", "AI agent workflow for Z". They watch tutorial and productivity channels.
- Formats available: screen recording with voiceover (primary), slides with voiceover, occasional talking head optional, Shorts cut from long-form.
- Assets on hand: paid AI tool subscriptions, a Claude Code and agent setup, documented workflows and policies, a public repository with security controls for AI agents, real job tasks from healthcare technology to use as worked examples (anonymized).
- Time budget: [hours] per week for research, production, and posting, by one person.
- Goal for the first 90 days: 30 long-form videos, Partner Program thresholds in progress, 2 affiliate relationships, 1 sponsor conversation.
- Constraints: no employer-internal data, patient data, or identifiable hospital systems; vendor sponsorships disclosed and never allowed to change a recommendation; no "this tool will make you rich" claims; every demo must be reproducible by the viewer.
- Rules: every post is a draft a person reviews and publishes by hand; no automated likes, follows, comments, DMs, or uploads; no tool receives passwords, cookies, or tokens; AI-generated or altered content gets the platform's label where its rules require it.
- Mark [Unverified] any platform threshold, policy, or price you cannot confirm, with the date of your knowledge.
```

## Niche-specific notes for all prompts

- **Category positioning:** metadata, titles, and descriptions should read as education (how to do a job), not gadget review. The measured RPM gap between the two categories is about 4x.
- **Mid-rolls:** keep core tutorials over 8 minutes; Shorts are for discovery only (3 to 14 percent of long-form RPM).
- **Affiliate order of priority:** tools with recurring commissions and a free tier the viewer can start with today.
- **Honesty signal:** every video includes one thing that did not work. It is the retention hook and the inauthentic-content defense at once.

## Prompts in recommended order

### Step 1. Niche evaluation

```text
Act as a YouTube market researcher and channel strategist.
Using the context above, evaluate "AI workflows for working professionals" as a niche. Cover: audience demand and search intent (name the top 20 search phrases you expect, grouped by job family), competition (name 5 to 8 comparable channels and what each does well and does not do), monetization ceiling (ads, sponsors, affiliates, products), content sustainability over 2 years as tools change, and the angle that makes this channel different given the owner's edge in healthcare technology and agent security.
Then define the ideal viewer in one paragraph, recommend 3 to 4 content categories, and give 30 video ideas ranked by expected pull for a new viewer, each tagged Short or long-form, with the tool, the job task, and the measurable result it should show.
Flag which ideas are tool-review templates that would blur into the inauthentic-content category, and replace them.
```

### Step 2. Channel plan

```text
Act as a YouTube strategist.
Using the context above, write a 12-week plan with:
- Positioning statement (one sentence) and a channel description under 1,000 characters.
- Content pillars with a target share: worked workflows (end to end, with results), tool comparisons on one real task, agent security and governance for professionals, and short Q and A on viewer problems.
- Formats and lengths: which pillars are 8 to 15 minute long-form, which are Shorts.
- A publishing cadence that fits the time budget, with hours assigned to research, recording, editing, and posting.
- Retention tactics for screen-capture tutorials: result first, then the build, then the failure, then the fix.
- A weekly action checklist the owner follows by hand.
Leave monetization out; it is a separate prompt for a later phase.
```

### Step 3. Scripts, hooks, titles, thumbnails

```text
Act as a YouTube content strategist and editor for a tutorial channel.
Using the context above, take the top 10 ideas from Step 1 and produce for each:
- 3 title options under 60 characters: specific, honest, state the task and the result, no "insane", no "secret", no all-caps.
- 1 thumbnail concept: a before-and-after or a single number (hours saved, steps removed) with an overlay of 3 words or fewer; no faces required.
- The first 15 seconds as a script: show the finished result, state the problem, promise the build.
- A section outline with the first payoff before the midpoint, the failure and fix after it, and a summary with the reproducible steps at the end.
- One line on why a viewer stays to the end.
Plain, concrete language. No em dashes, no "it's not X, it's Y" reveals, no engagement bait.
```

### Step 4. Analytics loop

```text
Act as a YouTube data analyst.
I will paste numbers I export by hand from YouTube Studio (impressions, click-through rate, average view duration, average percentage viewed, returning viewers, subscribers gained, traffic sources, top videos, and which videos drove affiliate clicks). You do not have account access and must not ask for it.
Build:
1. A weekly review template that takes 10 minutes: what to record, and what counts as signal versus noise at low view counts.
2. A monthly review template: which tools, job tasks, and formats worked, what to repeat, what to stop.
3. Decision rules, for example: low click-through rate with high average view duration means change the thumbnail or title, not the video; high drop-off before the first result means move the result earlier.
4. The three metrics that matter most in the first 90 days for a tutorial channel and why the others can wait.
State the sample sizes below which a conclusion is premature.
```

### Step 5. Growth mechanics

```text
Act as a YouTube growth strategist with expertise in content discovery.
Using the context above, explain how YouTube surfaces tutorial videos (search intent, suggested videos next to competitor tutorials, browse features, the Shorts feed) in plain terms, focused on what a one-person channel can act on.
Then give a practical growth plan: upload frequency within the time budget, how to test a title or thumbnail with few views, which Shorts to cut from each long-form video and how to link them, how to rank for "how to" searches while tools are new and competition is thin, and how to turn a viewer into a subscriber without begging.
Rules: every engagement action is manual by the owner. No automation, follow-for-follow, engagement pods, or paid views. Mark platform mechanics you cannot verify as [Unverified] with a date.
```

### Step 6. Production workflow

```text
Act as a production manager for a one-person screen-capture tutorial channel.
Using the context above, design a repeatable weekly workflow from idea to published video and Shorts cut from the same recording. Cover: task selection, script, screen recording setup (resolution, zoom and cursor emphasis, redaction of anything confidential), voiceover, editing, thumbnail, metadata, the AI-content disclosure step, and the human review and publish step.
Constraints: the publish step is always a person in YouTube Studio; do not propose auto-upload tools. Any tool you recommend must be named with its cost [Unverified] and must not need account passwords or session cookies. Include a quality checklist (reproducibility, no confidential data on screen, sponsor disclosure, affiliate disclosure, AI label if a synthetic voice is used) and estimated minutes per step so the total fits the time budget.
```

### Step 7. Monetization sequence (run after 30 videos or 90 days of data)

```text
Act as a creator business strategist specializing in YouTube monetization for software and AI education channels.
Using the context above and the analytics so far, lay out the revenue options in the order they become realistic: YouTube Partner Program (state current thresholds and mark them [Unverified] with a date), affiliate programs for the tools demonstrated (rank by recurring commission and free tier), sponsorships from AI and SaaS vendors (state a defensible CPM range and the disclosure wording), paid prompt and workflow packs, consulting, memberships, newsletter, a course only if viewers ask.
For each give requirements, pros, limits, and the trigger that says "now is the time".
Rules: sponsorships never change a recommendation and are disclosed on screen and in the description; affiliate links are disclosed; no income claims about the tools.
```
