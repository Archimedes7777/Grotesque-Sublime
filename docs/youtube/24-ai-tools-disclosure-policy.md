# Sponsor and affiliate disclosure policy

**Applies to:** the "governed AI workflows" channel and any other channel built from this folder. **Status:** draft for the owner to adopt before the first paid relationship. Written 2026-11-06.

**Legal basis, flagged for the owner's confirmation:** the United States Federal Trade Commission's Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising (16 CFR Part 255, revised 2023) require clear and conspicuous disclosure of any material connection between an endorser and a brand, including payment, free products, and affiliate commissions **[Moderate; confirm the current text at ftc.gov]**. YouTube separately requires creators to tick the "paid promotion" box on any video containing a paid placement, and its ad policies restrict certain product categories. Other countries have equivalents (the UK's CAP Code and ASA guidance, for example) **[Moderate]**. This policy is written to satisfy the strictest common reading and is not legal advice.

## 1. Principles

1. **Disclosure is clear, conspicuous, and early.** It appears in the video itself, spoken or on screen, before or at the moment the sponsored or affiliate content begins, and again in the first lines of the description. A disclosure only in the description, only behind "show more", or only in a hashtag does not count.
2. **A paid relationship never changes a conclusion.** If a sponsor's product loses a head-to-head, the video says so. If a sponsor asks for a change to a finding, the relationship ends.
3. **Only products the owner uses.** No affiliate link or sponsorship for a tool that does not appear in the owner's own workflow in the videos.
4. **No income claims.** Neither the channel nor a sponsor segment may claim or imply earnings from using a tool.
5. **The employer line.** No paid relationship with a vendor that supplies or bids to supply the owner's employer, and no use of the employer's name in any sponsored content. Check the employer's outside-activity and conflict-of-interest policy before the first deal and record the check.
6. **Separation from governance content.** Governance videos (vetting, permissions, incident handling) carry no sponsor segment. Their credibility is the channel's asset.

## 2. Sponsor acceptance criteria

| Accept | Decline |
|---|---|
| AI and productivity tools the owner uses on camera | Any product the owner would not use in their own job |
| Password managers, passkey hardware, security tools with public documentation | VPN services making privacy claims the owner cannot verify |
| Course and learning platforms with transparent pricing | Get-rich, trading, crypto, lending, or "AI business in a box" offers |
| Hardware and equipment relevant to the work shown | Supplements, health products, or anything requiring a medical claim |
| Professional training and certification providers | Any vendor that supplies or bids to supply the owner's employer |
| | Any sponsor that asks to review or approve a conclusion |

## 3. Disclosure wording

**Spoken, at the start of a sponsored video:**
> This video is sponsored by [name]. They paid for a segment, which I will mark when it starts. They did not see the video before you did and they did not change anything I conclude.

**On screen, at the start of the segment and for its whole duration:** "Sponsored segment: [name]" in the lower third.

**Spoken, when an affiliate link exists:**
> Some links in the description are affiliate links. If you buy through them I earn a commission at no extra cost to you. I only link tools I use in these videos.

**Description, first three lines, before anything else:**
> Sponsored by [name] (paid segment from [timestamp] to [timestamp]).
> Affiliate links: [tool names]. I earn a commission if you buy through them. I only link tools I use.
> No sponsor or affiliate changes what I conclude. Nothing here is medical, legal, or financial advice.

**When there is no paid relationship:** the description still carries "No sponsor on this video. No affiliate links." Viewers learn that the absence of a line means something, which protects the governance series.

## 4. Platform mechanics

- Tick YouTube's "My video contains paid promotion" box on every video with a sponsor segment or a product supplied free in exchange for coverage.
- Do not rely on that box as the disclosure; it is in addition to the spoken and on-screen disclosure.
- Affiliate links are disclosed in the description even when there is no sponsor segment.
- If a sponsor requires a dedicated video rather than a segment, the title carries "(sponsored)" at the end.
- Shorts cut from a sponsored video carry the on-screen "Sponsored segment" text if any sponsored frame is included; cut around the segment where possible.

## 5. Process before the first deal

1. Confirm the employer's outside-activity and conflict-of-interest policy in writing; file the confirmation.
2. Write the rate card from the measured median views of the last five videos times the niche sponsor CPM range (see `01-niche-research.md`, section 3.2), and quote integrations by median views, not subscribers.
3. Use a written agreement that states: the segment length and placement, that the sponsor has no approval over content or conclusions, the disclosure wording, the payment terms, and a termination clause if the sponsor requests a change to a finding.
4. Log every paid relationship (sponsor, date, video, amount, disclosure applied, paid-promotion box ticked) in a private register outside this repository. Nothing about amounts goes into a public file.
5. Review the register and this policy quarterly; revisit when the FTC or YouTube changes its rules.

## 6. Affiliate program rules

- Join only programs for tools that appear in the videos.
- Prefer recurring-commission programs with a free tier the viewer can start on.
- Never place an affiliate link in a governance video.
- Re-check each program's terms yearly; remove links for tools the owner stops using.
