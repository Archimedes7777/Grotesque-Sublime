# Launch plan: from "shop not live" to the first sale

**Status:** plan only. The shop is not live and no product has been ordered. Until each gate below passes, content stays brand and art only: no product, price, purchase-link, or scarcity wording (`.agents/product-marketing.md`).

**Principle:** audience and body of work first, products second, and scarcity claims only when they are literally true. Estimates are mine and tagged **[Estimate]**; prices and fees are **[Unverified]** until you check current vendor pages.

## Phases and gates

| Phase | What happens | Gate to continue | Time **[Estimate]** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Audience and library | Post consistently; build a library of finished pieces; learn what resonates | Four consecutive weeks of posting; at least 30 finished pieces; you can name the 3 to 5 pieces people respond to most | 4 weeks |
| 1. Foundations | Trademark knockout, domain renewal and security, handles secured, logo lockup decided, business and tax setup | Knockout results reviewed; `bobfrapples.com` renewed; logo decision made; accounts hardened | 2 to 4 weeks, in parallel with phase 0 |
| 2. Product validation | Pick 2 or 3 products; order samples; judge quality; choose a fulfillment partner | Samples you would be proud to ship; costs and shipping times known | 2 to 3 weeks |
| 3. Shop setup | Build the store, policies, checkout, support inbox | A test order completes end to end | 1 to 2 weeks |
| 4. Pre-launch | Collect emails with consent; tease without claims; prepare launch posts as drafts | Email list exists; launch calendar written; claims reviewed | 2 to 3 weeks |
| 5. Launch week | Publish the sequence; watch support and security | No open security issues; fulfillment tested | 1 week |
| 6. Review | Measure, learn, decide what to reorder | Review written | 1 week after launch |

## Decision: limited or open editions

The PDF's example says "limited to 50" and "no reprints". Decide before any marketing:

- **True limited edition:** numbered pieces, a fixed edition size recorded in writing, no reprints, and the edition size printed on the product page. Only then may you say "limited".
- **Open edition:** no scarcity wording at all. This is simpler and still honest.

Scarcity claims that are false can be treated as deceptive advertising. If unsure, ask an attorney.

## Shop platform options

| Option | Fit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Your own store (for example a hosted store builder) | Full control of brand and customer data | Monthly fees **[Unverified]**; more setup |
| A marketplace such as Etsy | Built-in traffic | Fees and rules **[Unverified]**; weaker brand control |
| Fulfillment partner's own storefront | Fastest start | Least control; check branding options |

Whatever you choose: turn on the strongest sign-in protection for the shop and payment accounts, and use a dedicated support email, not the brand identity-root email.

## Shop setup checklist

- [ ] Shipping, returns, privacy, and terms pages written (an attorney or a vetted template; do not copy another shop's text)
- [ ] Sales tax approach decided for your location and where you sell **[Unverified; ask an accountant]**
- [ ] Payment processor account with hardware-key or authenticator protection
- [ ] Support inbox and a response-time promise you can keep
- [ ] Product photos and mock-ups that show the real product
- [ ] A test order placed and received

## Pre-launch: email list

- Collect addresses only with clear consent, a plain description of what you will send, and an unsubscribe link in every email.
- Rules differ by country (for example the US CAN-SPAM Act and the EU GDPR) **[Moderate]**; check them for where your subscribers live.
- Teasers say what is true: "A shop is coming" is fine. "Limited to 50" is not, until it is real.

## Rewriting the PDF's ad copy

The PDF's ad copy assumes a live shop. Before any of it is used:
- Remove "limited-edition", "no reprints", and "collectible" until true.
- Replace "Shop Now" calls to action with "Follow for the drop" until the shop is open.
- Do not call mass-produced items "luxury"; describe the art and the craft truthfully.
- Keep "crafted with AI and human imagination" only if it describes what you did.
- Apply each platform's AI-content label to ads as well as posts.

## Risks

- **Rushing products before the audience exists:** produces inventory or listings nobody sees. The phase gates guard against it.
- **Trademark conflicts in apparel:** may force a rename or a narrower filing. Resolve before ordering apparel.
- **Return and quality problems:** damage the brand early. Order and inspect samples.
- **Scam surge at launch:** attackers target new shops. Follow the runbook and keep sign-in protection strong.
